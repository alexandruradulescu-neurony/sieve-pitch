the problem

A few ways this goes very wrong.

The silent vanish

200,000 rows in. The import says "success." 3,000 never made it. No error, no list, no idea — you find out in the quarterly report.

The 2 a.m. whack-a-mole

The import dies at row 87,412: "invalid input syntax for type integer." You fix it. It dies at 91,003. You fix that. It dies at 91,004. You are now hand-debugging a CSV, one row at a time, in the dark.

The Excel lie

It opened fine in Excel. But the delimiter is a semicolon, the encoding is Windows-1252, and there's a BOM. Your "clean" CSV is three surprises in a trench coat.

Sieve catches all three before they land.

Three horror stories, each a real failure class Sieve catches: silent data loss, cryptic per-row aborts, and files that look clean but aren't. Land the punchline: Sieve catches all three before they land.

the answer

Sieve.

The bad rows stay behind. The good ones pass through.

A CSV → Postgres importer that checks every row against your table before it lands — and tells you exactly why each bad one failed.

The one-line answer. Then the funnel: rows in, mesh checks them, good rows land in Postgres, bad rows get caught and filed. That's the whole product.

How it works

connect → upload → sift & load

01

Connect

Point it at your Postgres. Save it. The next import is one click.

02

Upload

Drop any CSV. Sieve reads encoding, delimiter, and the header row as it streams in.

03

Sift & load

It suggests the mapping. Bad rows get a report. Good rows bulk-load with live progress.

Three steps, three clicks to the demo. Connect once, upload, and Sieve does the sifting. Keep it moving — the demo is coming.
